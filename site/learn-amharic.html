Learn Amharic free: lessons, the Fidel alphabet and audio

The Amharic App is a free course in Amharic (አማርኛ), the main language of Ethiopia. It works on your phone or computer, even offline, with no account needed.

What's in the app

  • The Fidel. Learn to read the Ethiopic alphabet, letter by letter, with a sound for every letter.
  • 27 topic units. Each one is built around something you want to say, with notes, word lists, a dialogue and a test.
  • Audio everywhere. Tap a word, sentence or dialogue line to hear it, and slow it down if you need to.
  • Verb tables. Every form of more than 300 verbs, in fidel and English letters, each with its sound.
  • Numbers, time, money and dates. Including Ethiopian time and the Ethiopian calendar.
  • Reading stories and culture notes. Short graded stories and notes on everyday life in Ethiopia.
  • Tests, games and review. Spaced review brings back what you are about to forget, with a gentle daily goal.
  • Transcription switch. See every word in English letters too, like selam, until you can read the fidel.

The course, unit by unit

Beginner: units 1–10

Greetings, family, food and the town, with the present and past tenses.

  • Unit 1: Hello and goodbye. Saying hello · One “you”, or four? · Answering · Goodbye.
    Words include ሰላም selam “hello; peace”, ጤና ይስጥልኝ t'éna yist'illign “hello (more formal)”, እንደምን ነህ? indemin neh? “how are you? (to a man)”, እንደምን ነሽ? indemin nesh? “how are you? (to a woman)”.
  • Unit 2: Who are you? Names, jobs and where you're from. “I am”, “you are”: the copula · Your name · Where are you from? · Nationality · “Not”.
    Words include ስም sim “name”, ስሜ simé “my name”, ማን man “who?”, ምን min “what?”.
  • Unit 3: My family. My, your, his: endings on the noun · After a vowel · “This” and “that” · More than one: -ኦች · “Of”: የ · Adjectives come first.
    Words include ቤተሰብ béteseb “family”, እናት innat “mother”, አባት abbat “father”, ወንድም wendim “brother”.
  • Unit 4: Having and counting. “I have” · Having several things · “I don't have” · Numbers 1–10 · How many?.
    Words include አንድ and “one”, ሁለት hulet “two”, ሦስት sost “three”, አራት arat “four”.
  • Unit 5: Review of units 1–4.
  • Unit 6: Where is it? Around town. “The” · Where is…? · There is, there isn't · In, on, next to · Here and there.
    Words include ከተማ ketema “town, city”, መንገድ menged “road, way”, ባንክ bank “bank”, ሱቅ suq “shop”.
  • Unit 7: Coffee and food. “I want” · The object marker -ን · Not wanting · Food words · With.
    Words include ቡና bunna “coffee”, ሻይ shay “tea”, ውሃ wiha “water”, ወተት wetet “milk”.
  • Unit 8: Every day: the present and future. One tense for now and later · How it is built · Short verbs · “You” to a woman · Not · Going somewhere.
    Words include ዛሬ zaré “today”, ነገ nege “tomorrow”, ጠዋት t'ewat “morning”, ከሰዓት በኋላ kese'at behwala “afternoon”.
  • Unit 9: Yesterday: talking about the past. The past · Verbs ending in a vowel · Didn't · Was and were · Order of words.
    Words include ትናንት tinant “yesterday”, ትናንት ማታ tinant mata “last night”, ባለፈው ሳምንት balefew sammint “last week”, ሳምንት sammint “week”.
  • Unit 10: Review of units 6–9.

Intermediate: units 11–20

Shopping, travel and health, telling stories, and joining ideas with when, if, because and who.

  • Unit 11: At the market. Tens · Asking the price · Bargaining · Amounts.
    Words include ሃያ haya “twenty”, ሠላሳ selasa “thirty”, አርባ arba “forty”, ሃምሳ hamsa “fifty”.
  • Unit 12: Getting around. Telling someone to do something · Don't · Directions · From … to · When?.
    Words include ታክሲ taksi “taxi; minibus”, አውቶቡስ awtobus “bus”, ባቡር babur “train”, አውሮፕላን awroplan “aeroplane”.
  • Unit 13: How do you feel?. Hungry, thirsty, tired · “Has done” · It hurts · Happy and sad.
    Words include ርቦኛል ribognal “I'm hungry”, ጠምቶኛል t'emtognal “I'm thirsty”, ደክሞኛል dekmognal “I'm tired”, ታምሜአለሁ tamméallehu “I'm ill”.
  • Unit 14: Making plans. Let's… · To do: the verbal noun · I have to · Can · Meeting up.
    Words include እንሂድ innihid “let's go”, እንገናኝ innigenagn “let's meet”, መሄድ mehéd “to go, going”, መምጣት memt'at “to come”.
  • Unit 15: Review of units 11–14.
  • Unit 16: Telling a story: “and then…”. Joining actions · How it is built · The tense comes at the end · First, then, finally · How you did it.
    Words include መጀመሪያ mejemmerya “first, at first”, ከዚያ keziya “then”, ከዚያ በኋላ keziya behwala “after that”, በመጨረሻ bemech'ersha “finally, in the end”.
  • Unit 17: Tell me, give her: object endings on verbs. Me, you, him: on the end of the verb · The endings · In the present · Tell me, give me · For me: -ልኝ · Naming the person too.
    Words include ነገረ neggere “tell”, ጠየቀ t'eyyeqe “ask”, ረዳ redda “help”, ጠራ t'erra “call (someone), invite”.
  • Unit 18: When, if and because. When…: ስ- · If…: ከ- with the past · Because: ስለ · After and before · The order stays the same.
    Words include ለምን lemin “why?”, ስለ sile “because; about”, በኋላ behwala “after, later”, በፊት befit “before, earlier”.
  • Unit 19: The one who…: describing people and things. “Who” and “which”: የ- on the verb · In the present: የሚ- · The thing I bought · Not · Describing people.
    Words include ረጅም rejjim “tall, long”, አጭር ach'ir “short”, ወፍራም wefram “fat, thick”, ጥቁር t'iqur “black”.
  • Unit 20: Review of units 16–19.

Advanced: units 21–27

New verbs from old ones, work, phone calls, giving advice and opinions, and reporting what people say.

  • Unit 21: Making things happen: a-, as- and te- verbs. One verb gives you three · አ-: make it happen · አስ-: have it done, let · ተ-: be done · Learn them as words.
    Words include አቆመ aqome “stop (something), park”, አበላ abella “feed”, አፈላ afella “boil, make (tea, coffee)”, አሳየ asayye “show”.
  • Unit 22: At the doctor's. What's the matter? · Illnesses: አለብኝ again · Since when? · You'd better…: ብ- and ይሻላል · Taking medicine.
    Words include ምን ሆንክ? min honk? “what's wrong? (to a man)”, ምን ሆንሽ? min honsh? “what's wrong? (to a woman)”, ጉሮሮ gurorro “throat”, ጀርባ jerba “back”.
  • Unit 23: Travelling around Ethiopia. Booking a room · Buses · In order to: ለ- with the መ- form · I want you to…: እንድ- · Places to see.
    Words include ጉዞ guzo “journey, trip”, ጎበኘ gobegne “visit”, ለመጎብኘት lemegobignet “(in order) to visit”, ተከራየ tekerayye “rent, hire”.
  • Unit 24: At work. What do you do? · Right now: እየ- … ነው · Used to: … ነበር · A busy day · Looking for work.
    Words include ቢሮ biro “office”, ስብሰባ sibseba “meeting”, ደመወዝ demewez “pay, salary”, ሥራ አስኪያጅ sira askiyaj “manager”.
  • Unit 25: Review of units 21–24.
  • Unit 26: On the phone. Answering the phone · A bad line · He said…: quoting with አለ · That…: እንደ- on the verb · Leaving a message.
    Words include ሃሎ halo “hello (on the phone)”, ማን ልበል? man libel? “who's calling?”, አለ ale “say”, ይላል yilal “he says”.
  • Unit 27: Comparing and giving your opinion. Bigger than: ከ- · The most: ከሁሉ · Better: ይሻላል and ይበልጣል · I think: ይመስለኛል · Like and the same.
    Words include ይበልጥ yibelt' “more”, ከሁሉ kehullu “most of all, than all”, በለጠ bellet'e “be more than, excel”, ይሻለኛል yishalegnal “I prefer”.

Reading stories

Short stories written for learners. Each one uses only the words and grammar of the units before it, and every word can be tapped.

  • አዲስ ጓደኛ (New friends). Sara and Tom meet for the first time.
  • አልማዝ (Meet Almaz). Almaz shows us her family.
  • ዳዊትና ሳሙኤል (Who has more?). Two friends: one has a lot of things, the other has a lot of time.
  • በስልክ (On the phone). Dawit rings Sara with some good news.
  • ከተማዬ (My town). Yohannes shows us round his small town.
  • አያቴ ቤት (At Grandmother's). Coffee and lunch at grandmother's house.
  • የሐና ሳምንት (Hanna's week). What Hanna does every day, in the present tense.
  • ረጅም ቀን (A long day). Someone tells us about yesterday.
  • ገበያ ላይ (Bargaining). Samuel buys tomatoes and onions, and asks for a better price.
  • ባቡር ጣቢያ (The train station). Tom is looking for the train station.
  • ሐኪም ቤት (At the doctor's). Dawit doesn't feel well, and the doctor asks what he ate.
  • የመስቀል ዕቅድ (Plans for Meskel). Sara and Yonas make plans for the Meskel festival.
  • ቁልፌ የት ነው? (Where are my keys?). A morning that goes wrong, told with the “and then” verb forms from unit 16.
  • የልደት ስጦታ (The birthday present). Two brothers choose a present for their mother, with the object endings from unit 17.
  • ዝናብ ከዘነበ (If it rains). A trip to the lake, with “when”, “if” and “because” from unit 18.
  • ሠርጉ ላይ (Who's who at the wedding). Pointing people out at a wedding, with the “the one who…” forms from unit 19.
  • መኪናው ተበላሸ (The car broke down). A trip to Hawassa doesn't go to plan.

Ethiopia and its culture

  • Greetings take time. Why a hello can last a minute, and how to show respect.
  • Names and titles. No surnames: your name is your own name plus your father's.
  • The coffee ceremony. Coffee comes from Ethiopia, and making it is an event.
  • Eating together. One big tray, the right hand, and a mouthful from a friend.
  • Ethiopian time. The day starts at sunrise, so 7 in the morning is 1 o'clock.
  • The Ethiopian calendar. Thirteen months, and a New Year in September.
  • Holidays and holiday greetings. Meskel bonfires, Christmas in January, and the greeting for every festival.
  • Bargaining at the market. Friendly haggling, and the famous Merkato.
  • Minibus taxis. How to ride, and how to get off.
  • When someone is ill. Visiting, and what to say.
  • Proverbs. Amharic speakers love a good proverb.
  • The country at a glance. Where Ethiopia is, what the land is like, and a few key facts.
  • Regions and cities. A federation of regions, and the places every Ethiopian knows.
  • Peoples and languages. More than 80 languages, and the one most people share.
  • A short history. From Lucy to Adwa: one of the oldest countries in the world.
  • Faith. Ancient churches, ancient mosques, and a lot of fasting.
  • Injera, wat and more. The grain, the spices, the favourite dishes and the drinks.
  • Music and dance. Shoulder dancing, one-string fiddles and Ethio-jazz.
  • Running and sport. A barefoot marathon, a nation of runners, and football in every café.
  • Family and community. Big families, helpful neighbours, and saving money together.
